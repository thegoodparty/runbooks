## V. Public Hearings and Ordinances

### V.a — Ordinance 26-O-014: Animal Control Ordinance — Cat and Dog Limits and TNR Program (Third Reading) (pp. 12–24)

**Department:** Police Department, Code Enforcement Division
**Staff contact:** Captain Miguel Soto

**Background.** The purpose of the proposed ordinance change is to define the number of
dogs and cats a resident may own, to establish guidelines to allow a trap-neuter-release
(TNR) program in the community, and to make corrections to the dangerous-dog section of
the animal ordinance. The Code Enforcement Division has seen an increasing number of cat
hoarding cases. In the past 18 months, Community Service Officers have handled 4 cases
involving cat hoarding and have removed 96 cats. The proposed ordinance limits the number
of cats and dogs a person may own to six, with no more than four being dogs.

This is the **third and final reading**. The Council approved the first and second
readings at its April 21 and May 5, 2026 meetings. The ordinance takes effect 30 days
after final passage, on **July 16, 2026**.

**Legal citation.** The ordinance amends Lakemont Municipal Code Chapter 6, Article II,
Section 6-14 (Keeping of Animals) and adds a new Section 6-19 (Trap-Neuter-Release
Program).

**Committee action.** The Public Safety Committee voted **4-1-0** to recommend passage,
with Member Holloway dissenting.

**Staff recommendation.** Staff recommends approval of Ordinance 26-O-014 on third reading.

### V.b — Ordinance 26-O-015: Rezone 1.8 Acres at 410 Old Mill Road from Pre-Development (PRE) to General Business (GB) (pp. 25–34)

**Department:** Planning and Community Development
**Staff contact:** Planning Manager Theo Nakamura

This is a **public hearing** item requiring a vote. The Planning Commission held its own

**Staff recommendation.** Staff recommends approval of Ordinance 26-O-015 due to the
location of the property on a commercial corridor, its compatibility with existing uses,
and consistency with the Future Land Use Map.

## VI. Resolutions

### VI.a — Resolution 26-R-081: Authorizing a Notice of Intention to Issue Sewer Revenue Bonds (pp. 35–48)

**Department:** Finance
**Staff contact:** Finance Director Sandra Gillette

**Background.** To align with the Capital Improvements Plan (CIP), the City needs to issue
debt to finance essential sewer-system improvements. These bonds will fund collection-system
rehabilitation, lift-station replacement, and treatment-plant capacity upgrades. The
resolution authorizes a Notice of Intention to issue sewer revenue bonds in an amount not
to exceed **$42,000,000** in net proceeds, with a principal cap of **$43,500,000**. The
bonds are to be repaid **solely from sewer system revenues** and are not a general
obligation of the City.

Current recommended projects under this authorization:

- Riverside Trunk-Line Rehabilitation: **$18,400,000**
- North Lift-Station Replacement: **$9,250,000**
- Treatment-Plant Capacity Upgrade: **$11,100,000**
- Engineering, contingency, and issuance costs: **$3,250,000**

**Debt service.** Estimated annual debt service is $3,180,000 over a 20-year term. Staff's
financial advisor notes that the City's debt-service coverage ratio remains above the 1.25x
bond-covenant floor under this issuance.

**Staff recommendation.** Staff recommends approval of Resolution 26-R-081 authorizing the
Notice of Intention. Final bond terms will return to Council for a separate authorizing
vote in fall 2026.

## VII. New Business — Contract Awards

### VII.a — Downtown Streetscape Improvements Project — Construction Contract Award (pp. 54–60)

**Department:** Public Works / Engineering
**Staff contact:** City Engineer Paula Voss

**Background.** The 100 blocks of West 3rd Street and West 4th Street have undergone
several asphalt resurfacing projects over the last several decades and have surpassed
their useful life. The street will be reconstructed 40 feet wide (face of curb to face of
curb) with parallel parking and new sidewalks. The lowest responsive, responsible bidder
is **Cornerstone Civil Constructors, Inc.** of Riverton, in the amount of **$1,847,500.00**.
The engineer's construction cost estimate was **$1,902,000.00**, so the low bid comes in
**$54,500.00 under estimate**. The project has a completion deadline of **October 31, 2026**
and will be paid from a combination of the Downtown Tax-Increment Financing (TIF) district
and the Road Use Tax fund.

**Staff recommendation.** Staff recommends awarding the contract to Cornerstone Civil
Constructors, Inc.

### VII.b — Solid Waste and Recycling Collection Fee Adjustment (pp. 61–64)

**Department:** Public Works / Utilities
**Staff contact:** Utilities Manager Greg Aldous

**Background.** The current solid-waste collection and hauling contract was awarded to
Valley Disposal Services in January 2025. Per the contract's annual escalator tied to the
Consumer Price Index, the 12-month change in the CPI-U is 3.1%. The current fee of **$19.40**
per residential unit would be increased by **60 cents** to **$20.00**, effective **July 1,
2026**. The adjustment requires a vote.

**Staff recommendation.** Staff recommends approval of the fee adjustment.

## VIII. Community Center Rate Adjustments (pp. 65–70)

**Department:** Parks and Recreation
**Staff contact:** Parks Director Renee Caldwell

**Background.** The City Council approves daily admission and membership rates for the
Lakemont Community Center, as well as rental rates for the banquet rooms (the Lakeview
Room and the Heritage Pavilion). Staff proposes nominal increases across all rates. These
rates are typically increased a small amount each year. As staffing, utility, and supply
costs continue to rise, the rate increases will help offset these expenses. Even with the
updated rates, the Community Center's resident annual membership remains below the regional
average. The proposed adult daily admission rises from $6.00 to $6.50.

**Staff recommendation.** Staff recommends approval of the rate adjustments.
